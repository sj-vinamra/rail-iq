Built from the revised schematic. The VDSL extenders are deleted and the three com switches are joined on Cat5e, closed into a ring so a single broken cable leaves every car reachable the other way round. Use Break ring cable to watch the traffic reroute. The onboard equipment lives in one rack per car at the car end, with the vehicle supply items beside it in orange and the signalling interface items in M2 in magenta, the intercom panels sit beside the doors on opposite sides, and the driver console folds out of the front bulkhead with only the TETRA radio on it. The control centre never talks to a train directly, every path runs through the wayside network, the access point and the data transmission system. One NVR per vehicle is the stated fitment in the RAMS onboard equipment table. The figure of two appears in the spare parts list, Attachment A, and is a spares holding rather than a per train quantity.

One router per vehicle transmits. Right click a router and choose Transmit to move the air link to it, the others stay associated in standby.
Cables and couplers
Click a port, then a port on another device, to run a cable. Copper runs between RJ45 ports, fibre between SFP ports, audio between audio ports. A cable that leaves a car passes through every coupler in between and takes its contacts there.
